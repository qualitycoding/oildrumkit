# Oil Drum Kit — research record for the modal engine

Everything in `Source/ModeTables.h` and `Source/Voicing.h` is generated by `Tools/modes.py`
from `Tools/voicing.json`. This file says **what each number rests on**, with a confidence level,
so you know what to trust and what to tune by ear.

Confidence key: **A** = verified against a published value or measurement · **B** = standard theory,
checked only for internal consistency · **C** = my engineering estimate (no source) · **D** = pure voicing choice.

## 2. Standard theory, not independently validated

* **Donnell thin-shell frequencies** `Ω² = (1−ν²)λ⁴/(λ²+n²)² + β²(λ²+n²)²` for the side wall (simply-supported ends).
  Internal checks pass (n=0 sits at the ring frequency 2811 Hz; m=1 minimum at n=5, i.e. non-monotonic in n),
  but I did **not** find a measurement of a real drum shell to compare with. Real drums have rolling hoops
  that raise circumferential-mode frequencies, so shell partials are probably too low. **B**
* **Closed-cylinder air modes** (axial c/2L = 202 Hz, first circumferential 352 Hz, …). Textbook Bessel-zero
  results; the plan's open–closed tube (84/252/420 Hz) applies to a steelpan, not a sealed drum, and was replaced. **B**

## 3. Engineering estimates (no source) — tune by ear

* Damping law `σ(f) = 0.5 + π·0.0035·f + 1×10⁻⁶·f²` (η is inside the sourced 0.001–0.01 range; σ₀ and σ₂ are mine).
  Plan's original "T60 ≈ 8 s at 80 Hz" was rejected as unrealistic; with per-instrument `dampScale` the kit has
  T60 ≈ 1–2.5 s at the lowest mode and ≈ 0.3 s at 1 kHz for the drums. **C**
* Per-instrument `dampScale`, `splitPct` (0.15–0.5 %), `strikeR`, `shellAmp`, `cavAmp`, `nl`, `residual`, `pan`. **D**
* Hertz contact times 0.10 / 0.20 / 1.4 ms (metal / wood / rubber). Derived from `T = 2.87 (m²/(R E*² v))^{1/5}`
  with assumed tip radius, effective stick mass and moduli (e.g. wood tip on steel at 2 m/s ≈ 0.14 ms;
  an infinite steel plate of h = 1.2 mm presents ≈ 140 N·s/m, giving m/Z ≈ 0.14 ms — consistent).
  The 3–8 ms figures in the literature are for **drumheads** (membranes deflect) and do not apply to steel. **C**
* Snare wire model constants (resonances 2.6/4.1/7.3 kHz, Q = 40, impulse density 0.08/sample). **D**
* Cymbal "residual" noise bands: energy-density-matched to the top of the modal bank (derivation in `DrumEngine.h`);
  the overall level factor (`residual` 0.5–0.7) is **D**.

## 4. What could NOT be done, and what I did instead

* **Track B (real recordings) was impossible.** The development sandbox cannot reach freesound.org, so no
  real barrel/oil-drum audio could be downloaded and the plan's "lowest 15 analytic modes within ±8 % of
  measurement" check **was not performed**. The only empirical anchors are the two measured lid frequencies
  from US 6,339,960 (100 Hz and 250–280 Hz), which were *used to calibrate* the model — so they are not an
  independent validation.
* `Tools/modes.py analyse FILE.wav` is implemented (falling half-Hann window, peak-pick, band-limited
  Hilbert-envelope decay fit). Validated on synthetic ground truth: frequency error ≤ 0.01 %, decay-rate
  error ≤ 2.4 % for σ ≥ 12 s⁻¹ and up to 12 % for the slowest modes (bias from the finite window).
  **Please run it on your own recording of a real drum and compare with `modes.py report`.**
* ESPRIT refinement (optional in the plan) was not implemented.
* **No listening was possible.** All evaluation is spectral/statistical. Whether it *sounds* like metal
  struck by a stick is unverified by ear.

## 5. Corrections to my own earlier plan

1. Nodal-label "correction" I first announced was itself wrong; the plan's labels were right. (The plan's hard-coded ratio list is accurate for 15/16 entries within 1.4 %, but (1,2) = 13.00 should be 11.754, and its asymptotic extension law has ≈ 15–21 % median error — see `docs/RETROSPECTIVE.md` §4.1.)
2. Plate stretch factor: plan implied stretching (1.526); the measured lids show the ring *compresses* the (0,1) ratio for shallow-ring lids → default 0.8.
3. Cavity: open–closed tube replaced by closed cylinder.
4. Contact time: plan's 0.35/0.9/3.2 ms replaced by Hertz estimates 0.1/0.2/1.4 ms and sin^{3/2} shape.
5. Per-hit frequency jitter ±0.4 % → ±0.1 %: a fixed drum's mode frequencies do not change between hits; variation now comes from strike point/azimuth and per-mode level.
6. Mode-pair weights: plan's "opposite-sign cos" replaced by the physical cos/sin partition against a fixed per-instrument imperfection axis, with per-microphone azimuth gains (this also produces the stereo image).
7. Section A of the demo renders uses 3.0 s/hit (plan: 1.2 s) so tails can be analysed.
8. Cymbals/hats: 160 modes cannot cover 0.5–20 kHz at realistic density, so a modal bank (lowest kept mode at `hz`, ~35 Hz spacing, up to ~5 kHz) plus a decaying-noise residual above it was used.
9. Nonlinearity extended from cymbals to all drums (cubic acts on absolute level, so its effect ∝ v²).

## 6. Bugs found in the original engine (independent of realism)

* Output clips: every full-velocity bass/snare/tom hit peaks at **+4 to +6 dBFS** on its own; the demo groove peaks at +11.9 dBFS.
* Pitch smoothing was applied once per `process()` call rather than per sample: effective time constant ≈ 20 ms × block length (≈ 10 s at 512 samples).
* Ringing voices were live-retuned when the knob moved (glide) — not physical for struck metal (now behind `OILDRUM_LEGACY_GLIDE`, default off).
